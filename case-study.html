32% of revenue was hiding a 2.5% margin — and $87K of profit was being discounted away

A profitability and retention teardown of a four-year, $2.3M retail dataset. The brief: find where margin leaks, quantify it, and say what to change on Monday morning.

9,994 order lines2014 – 2017 793 customersPython · pandas RFM segmentationPublic sample dataset
Executive summary
  • One in five orders loses money (20.4% of 5,009 orders). The cause is not cost — it is discounting.
  • Orders discounted above 20% are 15.8% of revenue and -$135,376 of profit. Capping discounts at 20% would have recovered $87,539 — 30.6% of total company profit.
  • Furniture is 32.3% of revenue at a 2.49% margin. Tables alone lost $17,725 on $206,966 of sales.
  • 145 “at-risk” customers hold 21.5% of revenue at the highest margin in the book (15.8%) — a retention problem worth more than any new-customer campaign.

01Growth without margin

Revenue grew 51% from 2014 to 2017. Margin did not move: 10.2% → 12.7%, with a peak of 13.4% in 2016. The business got bigger without getting more profitable per dollar.

02The discount cliff

Margin does not decline gradually with discount — it falls off a cliff between 20% and 30%.

The number that matters: 1,393 order lines carry a discount above 20%. They generate $362,770 of revenue and -$135,376 of profit. Capping every one of them at 20% would have added $87,539 — 30.6% of the profit the company actually made.

03Where the money is lost

Furniture carries a third of revenue and almost none of the profit. Two sub-categories are structurally negative.

Read: Tables are not a “low-margin” product, they are a loss-making product sold at scale ($206,966 of sales). The fix is not “sell more tables” — it is repricing, bundling, or exiting the discount tiers that make them sell.

04Retention beats acquisition

Customer value is remarkably evenly spread — the top 10 customers are only 6.7% of revenue, so there is no single-account risk. The opportunity is in the middle: customers who used to buy and have gone quiet.

The insight: the “at risk” group (145 customers, 21.5% of revenue) has the highest margin in the book at 15.8% — above champions. A win-back sequence aimed at them protects more profit than the same spend on new acquisition.

05What I would change

1. Hard-cap discounts at 20% outside a named exception list
Applies to 1,393 historical order lines. Requires an approval step in the quote flow for anything above 20%.
+$87,539
2. Reprice or bundle Tables and Bookcases
Both are negative at current discount depth. Either raise list price to protect margin at the same discount, or exit the deep-discount tier.
+$21,198
3. Launch a win-back sequence for the at-risk segment
145 customers, 21.5% of revenue, 15.8% margin. A 10% recovery of that revenue is worth $7,814 in profit.
retention
4. Add a margin gate to the monthly reporting pack
Revenue-only dashboards let this run for four years. A discount-vs-margin view by rep and by sub-category makes the leak visible weekly.
prevention

06Method & data quality

Before any analysis: 9,994 rows audited, 0 duplicates, 0 columns with missing values, 0 unparseable dates. Date range 2014-01-03 → 2017-12-30. Discount bands are right-closed; RFM uses quintile scoring on recency, frequency and monetary value, with recency measured from the day after the last order in the dataset.

Honest labelling: this analysis uses the public “Sample Superstore” dataset that ships with Tableau — 9,994 line items, 2014–2017. It is a practice dataset, not a client engagement. Every number above was computed from the raw file, and the method is exactly what I would run on your order export.
